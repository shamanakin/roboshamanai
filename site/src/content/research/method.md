## Claim

The lab will not call a page decoded until these six moves have been applied, in order, and a person has signed the tier. The moves are a standard for this site. They are not a claim that the standard has been measured against other methods.

## Evidence

There is no outcome evidence in this draft. The six names are the working sequence named for DECODER in the Open Lab brief of 27 Nov 2026: anchor, observe, find patterns, build multiple paths, test coherence, distill. DECODER itself stays on its own site and is not reproduced here.

### The six moves

1. **Anchor.** Name the question, the source, and what decision the tier is for. If the question is about a person in care, stop. This lab does not publish clinical case material.
2. **Observe.** Record what the source actually says, separate from the gloss. Keep any quotation short and pointed at the source.
3. **Find patterns.** Group repeats. A pattern is not yet a conclusion.
4. **Build multiple paths.** Write at least two readings that could both survive step 2. If only one reading is possible, say why.
5. **Test coherence.** Drop the reading that contradicts the source, or another move. What remains is a candidate, not a fact, until the falsifier is named.
6. **Distill.** State the candidate in a few sentences, pick a tier (exploratory, provisional, or decoded), name the authorship (human, human and AI, or AI-decoded), and write what would prove it wrong.

A tier of decoded means the entry is signed. A draft, including this one, does not get that tier by default. This page stays provisional until it is reviewed.

## What would prove this wrong

This procedure is a bad lab standard if, on a public source, two careful passes do not produce a tier and a falsifier that can be signed, and the failure cannot be traced to a named move. It is also a bad standard if writers use it to launder a conclusion they had already chosen in step 1. Either result means version 1.0 should be withdrawn or rewritten, not quietly treated as settled.
